\section{A function and its domain}
Consider the function
\[
  f(x) = \frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}.
\]
Its original expression is defined for $x \ne 1$. Factoring the numerator gives
\[
  f(x) = \frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x-1} = x+1, \qquad x \ne 1.
\]

\section{A limit}
Although $f(1)$ is not defined, its limit as $x$ approaches 1 exists:
\begin{align*}
  \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}
    &= \lim_{x \to 1} (x+1) \\
    &= 2.
\end{align*}

\section{Continuity}
The function $g(x)=x^2+3x$ is a polynomial, so it is continuous for every real
number. In particular,
\[
  \lim_{x \to 2} g(x) = g(2) = 2^2 + 3(2) = 10.
\]
