\section{An antiderivative}
The power rule for integration gives
\[
  \int x^3\,dx = \frac{x^4}{4}+C,
\]
where $C$ is an arbitrary constant.

\section{Definite integral by substitution}
Evaluate $\int_0^1 2x(x^2+1)^2\,dx$. Let $u=x^2+1$, so $du=2x\,dx$.
The bounds change from $x=0,1$ to $u=1,2$:
\begin{align*}
  \int_0^1 2x(x^2+1)^2\,dx
    &= \int_1^2 u^2\,du \\
    &= \left[\frac{u^3}{3}\right]_1^2 \\
    &= \frac{7}{3}.
\end{align*}
